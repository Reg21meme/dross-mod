# The elemental knights (tools/knights)

Makes the frozen models of the elemental knights. In the user's plan (October 18, 2026, again November 7), two knights of
each of the five elements guard a broken portal in that element's biomes in the Overworld, and you fight them before you
ever reach the Dross. Ice already has its pair, the Thawed Soldiers (the Rimefall candidates 9C and 9D, `tools/rimefall`);
poison has Sir Mildew (No. 13 of the Blightwood candidates, `tools/blight`). The user asked (November 7) for three more
poison knights in Sir Mildew's style and four each for fire, thunder and earth, each with its own weapon and idea, so a
pair can be picked for every element. One gallery in the game, `/dross showcase knights`, shows all of them (the Thawed
Soldiers and Sir Mildew included), element by element in front of broken portals.

**Round 2** (November 7-9, after the user's look at round 1; round 1's fifteen are archived in `knightdesigns/round1/`,
their previews in `boss-previews/knights/round1/`): the user picked poison's pair (P1, P3) and earth's (E3, E4), kept F1's
look with other weapons and asked for a fire knight with only fire hands, and wanted thunder in the Dross's electric
blue, with T3 redesigned (he'll throw his anchor and pull it back) and a standard partner for him. A design module can
hold options of one design (`VARIANTS`, labels like "F1A").

**Round 3** (November 9, after the user's look at round 2; what was dropped is archived in `knightdesigns/round2/`, all
of round 2's previews in `boss-previews/knights/round2/`): thunder's pair is T3A ("T3A is the best") and T6 Stormblade,
F1C's twin blades given to "a speed ninja with lightning attacks"; F1B's flail became a fire hammer ("make the 2nd fire
dude the fire hammer"), so fire is F1A or F1B with F5. The Thawed Soldiers were made as tall as the knights (4.6 blocks
to their horns, `tools/rimefall/rimedesigns/d09_thawed.py`).

Each design module's `BLURB` gives its idea; it's in the Java list too, so the game shows it (hover over its button in
the chat, or right-click the model).

Everything is generated by Python, never drawn by hand, like the other candidates (see `BOSS_GUIDE.md`): the Rimefall kit
(`tools/rimefall/rimekit`: shapes, per-face UVs, the floating-piece and z-fighting checks, the previews), the fire kit's
painters and shape helpers (`tools/fire/firekit`) and the Blightwood kit's (`tools/blight/blightkit`, for the poison
knights, whose materials are Sir Mildew's). `knightkit/kbody.py` is the shared knight's body, about 4 blocks to the top of
the helm like the Thawed Soldiers and Sir Mildew: legs, hips and faulds, the breastplate, pauldrons, the arms in one of six
poses (`planted`: both hands on a weapon planted in front; `upright`: a polearm held upright in the right hand; `shoulder`:
a heavy weapon on the right shoulder; `shield`, `hang` and `raise` for the left arm), a cape, shields (heater, round),
hafts, a visor slit and `zigzag` (lightning or cracks). `knightkit/kmats.py` holds each element's palettes (never the
Dross's electric blue but for thunder): fire's blackened iron, lava and brass; thunder's blue-black steel, cobalt cloth,
silver and electric blue lightning (round 2; round 1's copper is still there for the archive); earth's stone,
deepslate, moss, amber and amethyst.

## Running it

From the project folder:

```
python tools/knights/build.py                    # everything (about a minute): files, previews, the Java list
python tools/knights/build.py --only F1 T2       # only these designs' files and previews (no Java list)
python tools/knights/build.py --quick <dir> F1   # quick sheets of these designs into <dir> (nothing in the project)
python tools/knights/build.py --board <dir> F1   # one picture per design: front, three-quarter, behind, at night
python tools/knights/build.py --previews-only    # previews only; the game files are left alone
python tools/knights/zcheck.py F1                # lists any face pairs that would still flicker
```

Needs Python 3 with numpy and Pillow.

## What it writes

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `assets/dross/geo/entity/knight/<id>.geo.json` | the model (per-face UVs) |
| `assets/dross/textures/entity/knight/<id>.png` | the solid parts (what you can see through is a hole here) |
| `.../<id>_glow.png` | what glows, drawn full-bright (eyes, lava, lightning, runes, poison) |
| `.../<id>_clear.png`, `_clearglow.png` | the see-through parts (crystals, glass, the Tidecaller's fin, flames) and what glows on them |
| `assets/dross/animations/entity/knight/candidate.animation.json` | empty (nothing moves) |
| `src/main/java/com/reg21meme/dross/boss/knight/KnightDesigns.java` | the generated list the game reads |
| `boss-previews/knights/` | a sheet each, `originals.png` (by element), `lineup.png` (real sizes), `index.html` |

The models are showcase-only: `build.gradle`'s `jar` task leaves `entity/knight/` out of the released jar, and
`boss/ShowcaseOnly` then doesn't offer the gallery.

## Changing a design

Edit its module in `knightdesigns/` (Bedrock units, 16 a block, y up from the feet, the front facing -z, its own left
+x), then run `build.py --board <scratch dir> <label>` to look, and `build.py` to write it. A sign line holds 15
characters (`NAME`, `MOOD`): the build stops if one is longer. Ids are lower case without underscores, and unique across
the elements.

## The real knights: mobs.py, props.py and ruins.py

The user's pairs (ice 9C and 9D, poison P1 and P3, fire F1B and F5, thunder T3A and T6, earth E3 and E4) are real mobs
in the game (`boss/knight/ElementalKnight`, CLAUDE.md step 18). Three more tools make their files:

| Tool | What it writes |
|---|---|
| `python tools/knights/mobs.py [<model> ...]` | Each knight's model on bones (its parts routed by `knightkit/kbody.py` and the designs' `s.part(...)`), its idle, walk and two moves keyed by hand: `assets/dross/geo|animations|textures/entity/knightmob/<model>.*`, the previews `boss-previews/knights/mobs/<model>_animations.png`, the cache `knightmobs.json` and the generated `boss/knight/KnightAnims.java` (each move's hit and release moments). Run one model at a time or all of them, never two runs at once (they share the cache). |
| (the animations) | Made in `knightkit/kanims.py` (November 18, 2026, the user: the first ones were "wonky and jittery and not fluid"). Its docstring says what made them jittery and how they're made now: smooth curves through the keys (no dead stop at each one), the body leading and the off arm and cape trailing, follow-through and a settle after a blow, seamless breathing idles, a walk made at each knight's real pace (`heavy`, `speed`, `walk_t` on each knight in `mobs.py`), 40 frames a second kept within 0.06 degrees. The moments in `KnightAnims` count the 3 ticks the game blends into a move (`MOVE_BLEND`: GeckoLib starts an animation only after its blend), so the blow on screen and the server's land on the same tick. |
| (arms that reach) | `knightkit/kreach.py` (November 19, 2026): each knight's arms bend at their elbows (`fore_r`/`fore_l`, split off by `mobs.split_arms`); two-bone IK puts a fist on a spot keyed from between the shoulders in arm's reaches; `two_hand_sweep` (the Cinder hammer, the Thawed axe, the scythe), `two_hand_chop` (the Geode pick) and `blade_cuts` (the Stormblade) are built on it. |
| `python tools/knights/audit.py [<model> ...] [--clip] [--smooth] [--joins]` | Checks the knights' animations (November 19, 2026), building them in memory: parts going into each other or the floor deeper than as they stand (plus `CLIP_TOL`, 1.6 units), snaps (a frame where a part's tip changes speed far more than round it, outside a blow's moment), the loops' seams, and the game's blends into and out of each move (an angle a turn or more off spins a part round). Exits 1 when it finds something. Run it after any change to `knightkit/kanims.py`, `knightkit/kreach.py` or a knight in `mobs.py`; six pass clean, the four with new two-handed or X cuts have small grazes and one-frame quickenings left (CLAUDE.md step 18). |
| `python tools/knights/props.py` | The Wreck Knight's thrown anchor as a model of its own (`knightmob/wreck_anchor`). |
| `python tools/knights/ruins.py [--only <element>[/<area>]]` | The ruin candidates: every biome area's own 4-5 layouts (100 in 21 areas; see below). Without `--only` it also writes the generated `boss/knight/KnightRuinDesigns.java`. `--no-nbt` writes the previews only. |

## Their ruins: ruins.py, ruinkit and ruindesigns

The user, November 18, 2026: "each biome should have its own unique layout ... in big caverns the rock layout ... in
big mountain areas a chance of lightning layout ... not big structures, just little areas where you fight these guys".
Each of an element's biome areas has its own module, `ruindesigns/<element>/<area>.py` (the areas and their order:
`ruindesigns/__init__.py`), with its `AREA` (name, biomes, setting, the showcase's ground, its palette) and 4-5
`@layout` functions, each building into a grid from the shrine kit (`tools/shrines/voxel.py`) and returning the two
knights' spots. The kit, `ruinkit/__init__.py`, holds the shared pieces (the broken frame, a frame lying flat, courts,
pillars, rubble, mounds) and, in its docstring, the grid's rules and the four settings: SURFACE (on the land), SHORE
(the back of the plot is the sea), CAVERN (walled in with rock under a roof in the showcase), MOUNTAIN (it builds its
own summit). Shared helpers of one element: `earth/earthkit.py`, `thunder/stormkit.py`, `poison/common.py`.

Every build checks each ruin and doesn't write one that fails: no whole frame (it could be lit), 6 or more frame
blocks, nothing floating, plants on their soil, no open lava, no water that can flow (but on a shore), both knights on
solid ground with room for their bodies (the build clears 3 x 3 x 5 round each). Output: the templates
`data/dross/structures/knight_ruin/<element>/<area>_<layout>.nbt`; previews `boss-previews/knights/ruins/<element>/
<area>.png` (the area's row), each alone in `<element>/<area>/`, `all_<element>.png`, `index.html`. Round 1's fifteen
(three layouts in each element's stones) are archived in `ruindesigns/round1/` and `boss-previews/knights/ruins/round1/`.

To add a layout: write a new `@layout` function in its area's module, add it to `LAYOUTS`, run
`python tools/knights/ruins.py --only <element>/<area>`, look at its picture, then run it without `--only` for the Java
table. A new area: its module, its name in `ruindesigns/__init__.py`.

## Their sounds: sounds.py

`python tools/knights/sounds.py` makes every sound of the ten knights from scratch (the Rat King's synthesizer,
`tools/ratking/ratkit/synth.py`, and the knights' own voice engine, `knightvoice.py`; nothing recorded, nothing from
Minecraft): each element its own character (ice: frozen breath, frost and cracking ice; poison: wet rasps,
bubbles, hisses and spores; fire: roars, embers and booms; thunder: hum, crackles, zaps and thunder; earth: grinding
stone, rumbles, gravel and crystal chimes), each knight his own weight and voice (`BUILDS`: the Wreck Knight the
heaviest, the Stormblade the lightest). Per knight: idle (2), a grunt as a move starts (2), hurt (2), death, steps, and
his moves' wind-ups, blows and releases. It writes `assets/dross/sounds/knight/<knight>/*.ogg`, its entries in
`sounds.json` (`entity.knight_<knight>.<name>`; a key twice is refused), the subtitles in `en_us.json` (keeping its
line endings), the generated `boss/knight/KnightSounds.java` and the page `boss-previews/knights/sounds/index.html`
(every sound with how long, bright and low it is, and a heavy-against-light table). A sound that hasn't changed isn't
written again (`sound_hashes.json`). `python tools/knights/sounds.py wreck,stormblade` remakes only those knights'
sounds; `--verbose` prints every sound's measures. In the game: `/dross showcase boss_sounds knights`.

**Their voices are no one's voice** (the user, November 18, 2026, after hearing the first ones, made with the
Huntsman's voicekit: "too much like the Rimefall dudes, especially use original audios"). `knightvoice.py` has no
throat and no vowels: each element's voice is made of its own stuff and rung in the knight's sealed helm (a bell's
out-of-tune partials and a tiny hollow room): ice a frozen corpse's breath rattling through ice, its ice groaning and
whistling through cracks (the axeman deeper, the spearman's whistles brighter); poison gurgling, bubbling and wheezing
(the Blight Warden thin, high and clicking, the Sporebound deep, puffing spores); fire a furnace's bellows and a
fluttering growl of flame (the Cinder Knight's deep and ringing like an iron furnace, Flamehand's hissing and flaring);
thunder the Wreck Knight's ship's hull groaning over a deep swell and a drowned hum through a broken radio, the
Stormblade's quick electric snarl (a buzz torn about by noise and ring-modulated, sparks); earth a cave's grinding
groan (the Geode Knight's crystals squealing like a rubbed glass, the Rockfall Knight's boulder rumble). Anything
pitched is kept irregular (every click of a creak or a rattle jittered, a buzz's pitch torn by noise, rings out of
tune), so nothing settles into a throat's comb of harmonics. `python tools/knights/voicecheck.py` measures it against
the Huntsman's and the Frostfolk's voices (periodicity, how far a comb of harmonics stands out, the vowel band); the
page and `sounds.py`'s output show the table: the Rimefall voices about 0.93-0.97 and 18-27 dB, the knights about
0.3 and 5-8 dB (white noise 0.11 and 3.8 dB).
