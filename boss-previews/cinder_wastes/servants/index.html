Fire servant candidates

Fifteen bosses for the Fire Necromancer's two open servant places, each an original look (A) and three variations. In the game: /dross showcase boss_designs cinder_wastes.

No. 1: Cinder Widow

A giant spider of charred wood, every joint of her eight long legs still glowing like a coal, an hourglass of lava on her belly and eight ember eyes; she strings smouldering webs across a mesa canyon over lava and drops on whoever brushes a strand. About 10 blocks across her legs.

No. 1A: charred wood

A: the original: charred wood, glowing knuckles.

No. 1B: obsidian

B: glassy obsidian veined with lava, spiked legs, a crown of spines on her back.

No. 1C: ash-silk brood

C: pale with ash, her legs wrapped in smouldering silk, three glowing egg sacs on her back.

No. 1D: molten belly

D: her belly a sac of see-through molten glass with lava churning inside it; longer, thinner legs.

No. 2: Brimstone Toad

A huge toad squatting in a sulfur pool among the Cinder Vents: its back a crust of cooled magma split by glowing cracks, warts that glow like coals, a pale sulfur-yellow belly, gold eyes with a bar of a pupil. The vents across its patch puff in time with its croaks; it swallows you whole. About 7 blocks wide.

No. 2A: magma back

A: the original: a magma-crust back, glowing warts.

No. 2B: sulfur crown

B: crystals of sulfur growing all over its back and brow, smoking vent holes between them.

No. 2C: throat sac

C: croaking, its head raised and a huge throat sac blown up under its chin, glowing through.

No. 2D: old horned

D: an old horned toad: a crown of obsidian horns round its head, spines down its back, scarred.

No. 3: Slag Scarab

A huge dung beetle, its shell bronze-black with a sheen like oil on metal, rolling a ball of slag that grows as it picks up ash along its track: it rolls the ball at you, and when the ball cracks, lava spills out. Break the ball and it goes berserk without it. About 4 blocks long, its ball 4 blocks across.

No. 3A: dung roller

A: the original: walking backwards, its hind legs up on the ball, its head down.

No. 3B: horned pusher

B: a great curved horn and two shoulder horns (like a Hercules beetle), shoving a smaller ball.

No. 3C: molten ball

C: its ball mostly molten under a cracking crust; glowing seams between the plates of its shell.

No. 3D: wings open

D: its wing cases raised and its see-through ember wings spread, the ball resting beside it.

No. 4: Heat Haze

A tall, gaunt figure of shimmering heat, barely there: a coal of a heart, hot threads running from it down its limbs, two white-hot slits for eyes, heat rising off it, a mirage pool shimmering at its feet. You see it on the horizon and it's gone when you get there; it leads you across its patch, and the fourth time it's real. In its fight it splits into copies: only the real one casts a shadow. About 5 blocks tall.

No. 4A: mirage

A: the original: all shimmer, threads of heat inside it.

No. 4B: sun-bleached

B: a bleached skeleton inside the shimmer, as if heat were all that's left of someone lost out there.

No. 4C: dust devil

C: from the hips down a whirl of ash and embers, a dust devil, instead of legs.

No. 4D: three copies

D: its trick in its fight: itself and two fainter copies beside it, each a little out of step.

No. 5: Wicker Effigy

A giant built by the Fire Necromancer's cult from bundles of charred logs bound with woven branches, a cage of bent rods for a chest with a prisoner inside it, a woven head with ember eyes under a crown of sharpened stakes, red ritual ribbons. It stands unlit in a clearing like a landmark until someone takes the offering at its feet; then it bursts into flame and walks, burning down as it fights. Free the prisoner before it burns up. About 15 blocks tall.

No. 5A: unlit

A: the original: unlit, as it stands waiting in its clearing.

No. 5B: burning

B: alight: flames on its crown, its shoulders and its hands, embers in every crack.

No. 5C: half burnt

C: late in its fight: its left forearm burnt away, bars of its cage burnt through, its head hanging.

No. 5D: kneeling

D: down on one knee, a hand on the ground, its cage close to you: the moment to free the prisoner.

No. 6: Glasswright

A hunched, soot-black creature with a long iron blowpipe for a mouth, a gob of molten glass glowing at its tip; round glass goggles for eyes, a furnace grate in its belly, a scorched leather apron, long thin arms holding its tools. It lives in a workshop full of glass statues; in its fight it puts up glass walls that turn the floor into a maze and blows bubbles of glass that trap you. Shatter its walls onto it. About 4 blocks tall, hunched.

No. 6A: blowpipe

A: the original: blowing a gob of molten glass.

No. 6B: glass-backed

B: its back bristling with shards of coloured glass it has grown (amber, bottle green, ruby).

No. 6C: four-armed

C: four arms: its blowpipe, tongs with a glowing gob, shears, and a finished vase held up to look at.

No. 6D: glass shell

D: its body half turned to glass: a see-through chest, its furnace heart glowing inside.

No. 7: Lamplighter

A very tall, thin servant who lights the way for the Fire Necromancer: a long charred tailcoat over a waistcoat with brass buttons, gloves, an oil can and a ring of keys at his belt, a lighting pole taller than he is; his head an iron lantern with a flame burning inside it, soot marking a face on its glass. He walks a road of braziers across his patch lighting each one; put one out and he comes for you. In his fight every lit brazier near him makes him stronger. About 7 blocks tall.

No. 7A: lantern head

A: the original: a square iron lantern for a head.

No. 7B: brazier head

B: an open iron brazier for a head, heaped with coals and burning; a coal scuttle in his other hand.

No. 7C: candelabra

C: three small lanterns on a branching neck like a candelabra; a bell hangs from his pole.

No. 7D: stilts

D: striding on tall wooden stilts, a ladder strapped to his back, the lantern swinging higher still.

No. 8: Kilnmother

A living kiln of fired clay and firebrick standing on stubby legs, cold and asleep in a mesa until you feed it: its open kiln door is its mouth, a grate of iron for teeth and the fire roaring behind it, two peep-holes glowing for eyes, coiled clay arms, smoke rising from its chimney. What fuel you throw in decides the fight (coal, blaze rods, a lava bucket); it belches hot firebricks and pulls you towards its door with a draught. Block its vents and it chokes. Beaten, it cools into a kiln you can use. About 6-8 blocks tall.

No. 8A: beehive kiln

A: the original: a domed beehive kiln.

No. 8B: bottle kiln

B: a tall bottle kiln, its belly round and its neck a long chimney; long arms.

No. 8C: pottery-laden

C: carrying racks of its own fired pots and amphorae on its back and sides, some cracked and glowing.

No. 8D: brick fortress

D: square and squat, firebrick bound with riveted iron bands, three stacks, hatches for eyes.

No. 9: Ashen Roc

A huge bird of prey, its feathers charred black with their edges still burning, a hooked obsidian beak, ember eyes. It nests on the tallest mesa pillar in its patch; take its glowing egg and it screeches out of the sky and hunts you anywhere in the patch. Its first flying fight: dives, gusts of embers, burning rocks dropped from above. Killed, it falls into its nest as an ash egg: crack the egg before it hatches again. About 7 blocks tall with its wings raised, 13 across them.

No. 9A: wings raised

A: the original: standing, its wings raised to threaten.

No. 9B: on its nest

B: settled on its nest of charred branches, wings folded, its glowing egg tucked in front of it.

No. 9C: bald vulture

C: a vulture's bare red neck and head over a ruff of ash, its wings spread wide to the heat.

No. 9D: flame-plumed

D: its crest, its tail and the ends of its wings burning as living flame.

No. 10: Ashen Smith

A hunched giant blacksmith in a forge cut into a cliff: bald and scarred, a riveted iron plate over one eye, a braided beard of iron wire, a scorched leather apron, forearms veined with old burns. You hear his hammer across the patch; he works with his back to you until you step onto his forge floor. His blows heat metal armour (the more iron, gold or netherite you wear, the more it burns); his quench troughs cool you. He forges a new weapon each stage: hammer, glowing tongs, a molten blade. About 6.5 blocks tall, hunched.

No. 10A: hammer & anvil

A: the original: at his anvil, his hammer raised over his shoulder, a glowing ingot in his tongs.

No. 10B: tongs raised

B: holding his tongs up to his eye with a red-hot ingot in them, his hammer hanging at his belt.

No. 10C: molten blade

C: his last stage: a blade of molten metal in his fist, his skin cracking open with heat.

No. 10D: bellows-backed

D: a forge on his back (bellows, a firebox and a chimney), a sack of coal at his hip.

No. 11: Pallbearer

The Pyre Procession: a slow funeral march that walks a loop of ash road across its patch, a drumbeat you hear from far away and a column of smoke you can see. At its head the Pallbearer: a gaunt giant of soot-black bone wrapped in grave-cloth, a bare skull with ember eyes under an undertaker's top hat, a long crape ribbon trailing from it; bent under a burning coffin chained to its back, censers smoking at its corners. Stop it and fight: at half health the coffin bursts and what was inside climbs out. About 8 blocks tall.

No. 11A: burning coffin

A: the original: the burning coffin chained to its back, censers swinging from its corners.

No. 11B: held overhead

B: the coffin held flat over its head in both hands, burning, like a pall carried high.

No. 11C: coffin burst

C: after its stage change: the coffin burst open on its back, empty, its lid hanging by a chain.

No. 11D: pyre drummer

D: beating a funeral drum on its chest, the pyre itself (a burning bundle of logs) on its back.

No. 12: Starborn

Cinderfall: now and then a red star streaks across the Dross sky, everyone in the dimension hears it land, and in its Cinder Wastes patch there's a smoking crater with a cracked, glowing meteor. Come close and the meteor splits: something rode it down. A lean creature of dark star iron, white-hot where it's still cooling, a long smooth head with three white-hot eyes in a row and a swept crest, white-hot crystals on its shoulders. Burning debris keeps falling in its fight; the crater stays as a landmark, with a new metal in it. About 6 blocks tall standing.

No. 12A: emerging

A: the original: pulling itself out of its split meteor in the crater, its hands on the rim.

No. 12B: standing free

B: out and standing, the meteor's shell broken into pieces round its feet.

No. 12C: still curled

C: still curled up inside the meteor, seen through a great glowing crack.

No. 12D: crawling out

D: low on all fours, long and lean, clawing its way over the crater's rim.

No. 13: Cinder Hermit

A giant hermit crab that has taken a toppled bronze bell for its shell (it rang over a chapel the Dross swallowed long ago), green with patina, a glowing crack down it. Its right claw is a huge lump of magma rock, its left a small picking claw; its eyes glow on their stalks. It hides in its shell (nothing gets through but a pickaxe), rams with it, and clangs the bell to stun you. About 5 blocks tall.

No. 13A: bronze bell

A: the original: a toppled bronze bell for a shell.

No. 13B: brazier shell

B: carrying a great iron brazier for a shell, a fire burning in it as it walks.

No. 13C: skull shell

C: living in the charred skull of something huge and horned, its eye sockets glowing.

No. 13D: urn shell

D: in a cracked funerary urn of painted clay, smoke curling out of its broken lid.

No. 14: Firestorm

A boss that is weather: in the Cinder Wastes' rare ash storms a spinning column of fire forms and wanders its patch, sucking up ash and charred logs and flinging them. No body and no lair: a funnel of fire about 11 blocks tall, scorched ground at its foot, and in its eye a molten core you can only hurt when the funnel touches down. It lifts off and sweeps round between.

No. 14A: fire column

A: the original: a funnel of fire, its molten core inside.

No. 14B: ash storm

B: an ash storm: a funnel of grey ash and smoke shot through with embers, fire only at its foot.

No. 14C: twin funnels

C: two funnels of fire twisting round each other, sharing one core between them.

No. 14D: eye of storm

D: the eye of the storm: its core a molten face with two great eyes, looking out through the fire.

No. 15: Lanternwing

A giant moth that hunts light: in its patch every torch, lantern or glowstone you put down fills a hidden meter, and when there's too much light it comes out of the dark. Its wings are ember scales, ash grey and burnt orange, with two great glowing eyespots that look like a face; its body is thick with ash fur, its antennae feathery. It dives at the brightest light near you (your own torches become bait), sheds burning scales and throws dust that blinds. Put your lights out to lose it. About 10 blocks across its wings.

No. 15A: wings spread

A: the original: its wings raised, the eyespots glaring.

No. 15B: wings folded

B: resting, its wings folded over its back like a roof, the eyespots looking out from their sides.

No. 15C: glass-winged

C: a clearwing: see-through wings of smoky glass with glowing veins and dark rims; a banded body.

No. 15D: lantern belly

D: its abdomen a lantern, glowing through a cage of chitin; darker wings, smaller eyespots.
